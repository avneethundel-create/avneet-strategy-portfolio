A step by step framework for a product launch plan

How to build a launch plan that the whole company can tell

Most launch decks list features. This framework starts with pre work, then the gap between what the product team sees and what the market feels, and builds every message, asset and metric from there. Each section explains what it does, how to write each part, and the AI prompts that get you there faster.

It's designed around a single feature launch. If you're launching several features, section 3 shows how to turn them into one story. Worked examples come from the AI Signals launch plan I wrote at Firmable in June 2026.

Pre work · before the deck

Do the pre work first

Most launches go wrong before anyone writes a message. The pre work decides how big the launch is, who it's for and what customers actually say. Skip it and the deck ends up describing features in the product team's words.

1

Set the goal and the launch tier

Why it matters

Not every release deserves the same effort. Sizing the launch first stops you over investing in small updates or under investing in big ones.

How to do it

Agree one business goal with product and leadership. Then pick a tier. Tier 1 is a major launch with the full internal and external rollout. Tier 2 is enablement plus core channels. Tier 3 is release notes, in product messaging and a CS update.

Output

One goal sentence, a tier, and which sections of this framework you'll use.

2

Define who it's for

Why it matters

A launch aimed at everyone lands with no one. New prospects and existing customers need different messages.

How to do it

Name the primary buyer and the main user. Split the audience into prospects, existing customers on the right plan, and customers who'd need to upgrade. Rank which matters most for the goal.

Output

A short audience list with a priority order and the persona you'll write for.

3

Listen to customers

Why it matters

The pains in section 2 must come from the market, not the roadmap. Customer words make every later message sharper.

How to do it

Review call recordings and transcripts, run a working session with CS and two or three AEs, read win and loss notes, and if you can, interview three to five customers. Capture exact phrases, not summaries.

Output

A list of top frustrations in customers' own words, ranked by how often they come up.

4

Scan the competition

Why it matters

Buyers will compare you whether you mention competitors or not. You need to know what they claim before you write your difference.

How to do it

List the three to five alternatives buyers use today, including spreadsheets or doing nothing. For each, note what they claim, where they're genuinely strong and where buyers complain.

Output

A one page competitive snapshot that feeds the message house and the battlecard.

5

Confirm pricing and packaging

Why it matters

Sales will be asked what it costs on day one. If the answer isn't settled, the launch stalls in the first call.

How to do it

Decide whether the feature is included in existing plans, sold as an add on, or part of a new tier. Agree how sales should talk about it and which upgrade path you want to drive.

Output

One line on packaging and one on how sales positions it. At Firmable, the features were included in existing plans, which made the launch an upgrade moment.

6

Line up customer proof

Why it matters

A launch with a customer quote is far more believable than one without. Proof takes weeks to secure, so start early.

How to do it

Find two or three beta or early customers. Ask for a quote, a before and after number, or permission for a short case study.

Output

At least one named or anonymised customer story ready for launch day.

7

Check readiness and owners

Why it matters

Messaging can't fix a product that isn't ready. Clear owners stop tasks falling between teams.

How to do it

Confirm the launch date with product, the release type (beta or general availability) and known limitations. Make sure help docs and support are ready. Give every workstream one owner.

Output

A readiness checklist and an owner list covering product, marketing, sales, CS and support.

AI prompts for pre work
Set the context (start every session with this)

You're helping me plan a product launch. Here's the context. Product and feature: [what's launching] Who buys it: [role, company size, industry] Market: [region] What buyers use today instead: [alternatives] Call notes and customer quotes: [paste] Before you write anything, ask me up to five questions about what's missing.

Pick the launch tier

Here's what we're releasing and our business goal [paste]. Recommend a launch tier (1 major, 2 meaningful, 3 minor), explain why in three sentences, and list which activities each tier should include.

Define the audience

Based on this product and customer list, split our audience into prospects, existing customers on eligible plans and customers who would need to upgrade. For each, say what they care about most and which persona to write for first.

Find customer language

From these transcripts and notes, list the ten frustrations customers mention most about [problem area]. Quote their exact words and count how often each one comes up.

Scan the competition

For each of these alternatives [list], summarise what they claim, where they're genuinely strong, and the most common complaints from buyers. Present it as a table, then tell me where the gap is that we can own.

Position the packaging

[Feature] will be [included in plans / an add on / a new tier]. Write a two sentence explanation sales can use when a prospect asks what it costs, and suggest the upgrade path we should push.

Ask for customer proof

Write a short, friendly email asking a beta customer for a quote or a before and after metric about [feature]. Make it easy to say yes, with two example quotes they can edit.

Build the readiness checklist

Create a launch readiness checklist for [feature] covering product, docs, support, sales, CS and marketing, with one owner and a due date per item.

Section 01

Name the launch

Names the launch and sets the scope. Keep it simple. The real story starts in the next section.

1

Launch name and scope

What it does

Tells the room what's launching and that the plan covers both the internal and external rollout.

Formula

[Feature name] Internal and External Launch Plan · [Month Year]

Example

Name the launch

Section 02

The gap

This is the most important section of the deck. It shows the divergence between what the product does and what the market actually feels, and reframes the launch as solving a problem the market already has.

Firmable example

Signals was built to turn buying triggers into pipeline. But spotting the signal is only half the story.

The product team sees adoption, coverage, and workflow gaps. The market feels something different; missed pipeline, invisible opportunities, and signals that never translate to action. This bridges that gap. Because we are not launching features. We are solving a problem the market already has.

Manual agent set up

Sales teams are leaving pipeline on the table because the tools that should surface buying signals are too complex to actually use.

Invisible opportunities

Most sales teams are only prospecting within accounts they already know. The best opportunity is often the company they haven't found yet.

Delay to action

Knowing when to reach out is only half the battle. If acting on that signal requires switching tools and manual work, most reps simply won't.

The ResultPipeline lost to complexity. Opportunities never found. Buying moments that passed before anyone acted. These are not product problems. They are revenue problems. And they compound each other every day.
1

The headline highlights the gap

What it does

States what the product was built to do, then admits what's still missing. That tension is the reason the launch exists.

Formula

[Product] was built to [intended outcome]. But [the part still broken] is only half the story.

Example

FirmableSignals was built to turn buying triggers into pipeline. But spotting the signal is only half the story.

2

The sub line shows the divergence

What it does

Puts the product team's view next to the market's view. Product talks in metrics. The market talks in losses. Then it reframes the launch as solving a problem, not shipping features.

Formula

The product team sees [internal metrics or gaps]. The market feels something different; [pain 1], [pain 2], and [pain 3]. We are not launching features. We are solving a problem the market already has.

Example

FirmableThe product team sees adoption, coverage, and workflow gaps. The market feels something different; missed pipeline, invisible opportunities, and signals that never translate to action.

3

Key market pain points

What it does

Names the current market pain in two or three words, then explains it in one sentence. Three pains is ideal. One or two is fine for a small launch.

Formula

Label · 2 or 3 words a buyer would say
Sentence · [Who] are [losing what] because [the cause].

Example

FirmableManual agent set up. Sales teams are leaving pipeline on the table because the tools that should surface buying signals are too complex to actually use.

  • If the label describes a feature, rewrite it. It should describe a problem.
  • Use words you've heard on customer calls. Buyers never say "workflow gaps."
  • Every sentence should name a loss, like pipeline, time or revenue.
4

The result

What it does

Turns the pains into business language. This is what gets leadership to care. These aren't product problems, they're revenue problems.

Formula

[Pain 1 as a loss]. [Pain 2 as a loss]. [Pain 3 as a loss]. These are not product problems. They are revenue problems. And they compound each other every day.

Example

FirmablePipeline lost to complexity. Opportunities never found. Buying moments that passed before anyone acted.

AI prompts
Find the pain in customer language

From these call notes, list the ten frustrations buyers mention most about [problem area]. Quote their exact words and count how often each comes up.

Name the pains

Group these frustrations into three market pains. Name each in two or three words a buyer would actually say. Then explain each in one sentence that says who is affected, what they lose and why.

Write the gap

Write one sentence on what our product team measures today (adoption, usage, gaps) and one on what the market actually feels. Make the contrast sharp. End with a line that reframes this launch as solving a problem the market already has.

Write the title

Write five versions of a headline in this format. "[Product] was built to [intended outcome]. But [what's still missing] is only half the story." Keep each under 20 words.

Write the result

Rewrite these three pains as short business losses, one fragment each. Then add two sentences explaining why they're revenue problems, not product problems.

Check before moving on
Section 03

The solution

Now, and only now, introduce the product. Each pain from section 2 gets matched to how the product solves it, with the benefit written as an outcome.

Firmable example

Three features. One engine. Built to find, watch, and act cohesively.

AI Signals finds the right companies, at the right moment, and routes action directly to the rep

Feature 1 · Signal TemplatesMore pipeline from net new accounts

A gallery of preconfigured sales templates. Pick a trigger that matches your motion, activate it, and signals start working immediately. No configuration needed.

Feature 2 · Market SignalsMake the invisible visible

Watch the whole market, not just your list. Agents scan every company in the database, surfacing net new prospects the moment they show a buying trigger.

Feature 3 · Signal Agent ActionsFaster rep response to buying triggers

Buying triggers push directly to your CRM. No copying and pasting, or remembering to check signals. Be notified instantly.

1

Map every pain to a solution

What it does

Proves the product answers the exact problems in section 2. If a pain has no matching solution, drop the pain or flag the gap.

For a one feature launch

Use one feature and break it into three capabilities, each mapped to one pain.

Example mapping

FirmableManual set up → Signal Templates
Invisible opportunities → Market Signals
Delay to action → Agent Actions

2

Benefit headline, then description

What it does

Leads with what changes for the buyer. The feature name is the label, not the headline.

Formula

Headline · the outcome in 6 words or fewer
Description · what it does, then what the user no longer has to do.

Example

FirmableFaster rep response to buying triggers. Buying triggers push directly to your CRM. No copying and pasting, or remembering to check signals.

Launching more than one feature? Make it one engine.

Three separate feature drops sound like three small updates. One engine sounds like a product transformation. Give the features one shared headline, one line that says what they do together, and make each feature solve one pain. The Firmable plan put it this way. "This is not three separate feature drops; it is one product transformation that closes every gap that was holding Signals back."

[Number] features. One engine. Built to [verb], [verb], and [verb].

AI prompts
Map pains to features

Here are our three market pains and the product capabilities we're launching. Match each pain to the capability that solves it and explain how in one sentence. Flag any pain we don't actually solve.

Write benefit headlines

For each capability, write three benefit headlines of six words or fewer that describe the outcome for [buyer], not the feature.

Make it one engine

We're launching [number] features. Write a headline in the format "[Number] features. One engine. Built to [verb], [verb], and [verb]." Then write one sentence describing what the features do together.

The components

What it does

Organizational outcomes

What it does

What the buyer's business gets. One headline of three short sentences, then one outcome under each component.

Formula

Example

The difference

What it does

Why you and not the alternative. Acknowledge what alternatives offer, then say what only you give.

Formula

6

Delivered responsibly

What it does

The trust proof points buyers ask about before they buy, especially for AI. Accuracy, security and fit for their market.

Formula

Three proof points buyers will check, like accuracy, security, compliance or local fit.

AI prompts
Write the conflict

Compress these three market pains into one sentence that [buyer] would agree with on a call. Format it as "[Who] are [losing what] because [root cause]." Give me five options.

Write the answer

Write one sentence that starts with "[Product] now" and uses three verbs, one for each of these components. Keep it under 25 words.

Write the outcomes

Write an outcomes headline in the format "More [x]. Less [y]. Faster [z]." Then write one short outcome statement for each component.

Write the difference

Our alternatives are [list]. Write our difference as two short sentences. The first says what alternatives give buyers. The second says what we give that they can't, with one proof point.

Find the trust proof points

List the five concerns [buyer] in [industry] raise most before buying an AI product. Then pick the three we can prove and write each as a short proof point.

Pressure test it

Here's our message house. Act as a skeptical [buyer]. Tell me which line you don't believe, which is too vague, and which sounds like every other vendor.

Section 05

Arm the team before anything goes external

A launch without internal alignment is just an announcement. This section makes sure every rep tells the same story, handles objections confidently and has the assets they need before the first external message goes out.

1

Build the collateral, pre work first

  • Pre work. A CS and sales working session. Run a session with CS and two or three AEs to gather real objections and customer language before building anything.
  • Message house. One page. The conflict, the answer, the pillars, the outcome and the difference.
  • Battlecard. How the product compares with alternatives and what to say when a prospect names a competitor. One page reps will actually use.
  • Seller placemat and demo talk track. A cheat sheet for before and during calls, plus a demo that tells every feature as one story.
  • One home for everything. Centralise every asset where reps already work, like Notion or Confluence.
2

Run a GTM enablement call

Who joins

SDRs, AEs, sales leadership, CS leads and marketing.

What to cover

The narrative, why customers should care with each feature framed as a problem solved, the top objections, and where every asset lives.

The test

Every rep leaves the call able to tell the same story.

Firmable objection examples"We already have signals, we don't use it." These features removed the exact reasons you weren't using it.
"Will this create CRM noise?" Reps control which signals trigger actions and configure agents to match their workflow.

AI prompts
Mine the working session

Here are notes from our working session with CS and sales. Pull out every objection and the exact customer language behind it. Group them by theme and rank by how often they came up.

Build a battlecard

Write a one page battlecard for [product] against [competitor]. Include when they come up, where they're genuinely strong, where we win, one trap question to ask, and one thing reps should never say.

Write the demo talk track

Write a five minute demo talk track that opens with the conflict from our message house, shows each feature as the answer to one pain, and ends with the outcome. Tell it as one story, not a feature tour.

Handle objections

Write a response to each of these objections in under 25 words. Acknowledge the concern first, then reframe it using our message house.

Section 06

Training, readiness and the two week rollout

Every rep practises the story before the market hears it, and nothing goes external until every box is checked.

The rollout

Two week internal rollout

Day 1 to 2Collateral built and live in one home
Day 3 to 5Working session with CS and AEs
Day 5GTM enablement call
Day 6 to 9Sales training complete, FAQ updated
Day 10 to 14Go or no go. External launch begins

After launch · a debrief to assess performance and find improvements

1

Sales training

  • One story, every rep. Every SDR and AE practises the talk track before external launch. Call blitzes and AE sessions work well for this.
  • Partner with sales on a short training module explaining the new capability and its value for customers.
  • Update the internal FAQ with new capabilities, key messages and the questions customers will ask.
2

Launch readiness check

  • All collateral QA checked so every asset tells the same story.
  • The asset hub is live and reps can find what they need without chasing the PMM.
  • Go or no go. If collateral isn't signed off or training is incomplete by day 9, the external launch moves by a few days. Internal readiness comes first.
AI prompts
Write the internal FAQ

Write an internal FAQ for [feature] with the ten questions reps and CS will be asked most. Keep each answer under 40 words and consistent with this message house.

Build the rollout checklist

Turn this launch plan into a two week rollout checklist with an owner and due day for every task, plus clear go or no go criteria for day 9.

Role play the call

Act as a [buyer] on a discovery call. I'm the rep pitching [feature]. Raise realistic objections one at a time and score my answers against our message house.

Section 07

External launch. One moment, not separate announcements.

Every channel is anchored to one narrative, written as one line every asset ladders back to.

1

Digital and content

  • LinkedIn launch post. Problem led, not feature led. Open with the pain, then show how the product solves it end to end.
  • Demo video. A short "why we built this" walkthrough that shows rather than tells.
  • Blog post and website. Written from the pain angle, with the website updated to match.
  • Email to existing customers. Personal to what changed for them, not a generic product update.
  • In product notification. Surface the feature at the moment users are most likely to try it.
2

Demand gen

  • Outbound sequences. Give stalled prospects a timely reason to re engage.
  • Paid social. Boost the launch post or run a short campaign aimed at your buyers.
3

One narrative across all channels

What it does

Keeps every subject line, post and ad telling the same story.

Formula

[Verb] the [thing], [verb] the [moment], [verb] [how].

Example

FirmableFind the right companies, spot the right moment, act automatically.

AI prompts
Write the narrative line

Using this message house, write five one line narratives that every launch asset can ladder back to. Use three short verb phrases.

Write the launch post

Write a LinkedIn launch post for [feature]. Open with the pain in the buyer's words, not the feature. Show how it's solved end to end in three short lines, then end with one clear next step. Under 180 words.

Write the customer email

Write an email to existing [plan] customers explaining what changed and why it matters for their workflow. Make it specific to how they use the product today. Under 150 words.

Write the outbound sequence

Write a three touch outbound sequence that uses this launch as a reason to re engage prospects who went quiet. Each email under 80 words, problem first.

Section 08

Webinars, partnerships and events

Webinars and events put the product in front of the right people at the right time. Partnerships multiply reach through channels you don't own.

1

Webinars, one per audience

  • Product spotlight for prospects. A live end to end demo positioned as a first look.
  • Customer webinar. What changed, why it matters and how to get more from it. Drives adoption and reduces churn risk.
2

Partnerships

  • Amplify through partner channels and communities where your buyers already are.
  • Lead with the most relevant feature. At Firmable, the CRM integration was the strongest story for HubSpot and Salesforce audiences.
  • Brief partners with key messages and assets so they tell the same story.
3

An event, if the product is demonstrable

A small invite only demo for customers and warm prospects. It's usually more realistic in week 3 or 4 than on launch day, and doubles as an expansion opportunity for CS.

AI prompts
Plan the webinar

Outline a 30 minute product spotlight webinar for [buyer]. Open with the problem, demo the workflow end to end, include one customer story and end with a clear next step.

Pick the partners

We sell [product] to [buyer] in [market]. List the ten partner channels, communities and newsletters where these buyers already spend time, and which feature to lead with for each.

Section 09

How we measure success, tactical to strategic

Tactical metrics tell you if the launch executed well. Operational tells you if the product is being used. Strategic tells you if it drove revenue. Tracking all three shows exactly where the launch is working, so you can course correct fast.

Firmable example

Strategic · did it drive revenue?15 to 20plan upgrades in 60 daysOwner · Marketing, Sales and CS
1

Three tiers, one owner each

Tactical

Did the launch land? Collateral usage, training completion, launch post reach.

Operational

Is it being adopted? Feature activation, usage by segment, time to first value.

Strategic

Did it drive revenue? Upgrades, expansion, new pipeline, win rate.

2

The ROI signal to watch

Name the one commercial signal sales should track for 60 days. At Firmable, all three features were included in existing plans, so the launch didn't create a new paid tier. It raised the value of the one that already existed. The signals to watch were plan upgrades from lower tiers and activation inside existing accounts, because those cohorts showed intent for expansion conversations.

AI prompts
Set the metrics

Suggest one tactical, one operational and one strategic metric for launching [feature]. Give each a realistic target based on these baselines [paste], a time frame and one owning team.

Find the commercial angle

[Feature] is included in [plans]. Explain how this launch could still drive revenue, and which two signals sales should track in the 60 days after launch.

Final check before you present
After launch

Listen, learn and update

Launch day is the start, not the finish. The first 30 to 60 days tell you whether the message is landing, and the best teams update it fast.

1

Check the message is landing

Why it matters

What you wrote and what reps say on calls drift apart within weeks.

How to do it

After 30 days, listen to launch related calls. Are buyers repeating your conflict line back? Are reps using the talk track or reverting to features?

Output

A short list of lines to keep, cut or rewrite.

2

Close the feedback loop

Why it matters

Sales and CS hear objections you never planned for.

How to do it

Collect new objections, win and loss reasons on launch influenced deals, and feature requests. Update the message house, battlecard and FAQ.

Output

Updated assets, shared with the team with a note on what changed.

3

Run the debrief

Why it matters

Each launch should make the next one easier.

How to do it

Compare results against every metric, note what worked and what didn't, and agree three changes for next time. Share results with the whole company.

Output

A one page debrief and a company update celebrating early wins.

AI prompts
Is the message landing?

Here are transcripts from calls since launch [paste]. Which lines from our message house are buyers repeating back, which are they ignoring, and what new objections are coming up? Suggest three edits to the message house.

Plan the debrief

Write a one page post launch debrief that compares results against each metric, lists what worked and what didn't, and ends with three changes for the next launch.

Share the wins

Write a short internal update celebrating the launch results so far. Name the teams involved, lead with one customer story, and end with what's next.
